“A closed mouth doesn’t get fed.”

That’s something my father has told me for as long as I can remember — and it’s a mindset that’s helped shape my growth from a timid person into someone who’s vocal and seeks out opportunities.

Over the years, I’ve learned how much simply initiating a conversation can make a difference in situations where many people might hesitate. Using my voice more often to express my interests, goals, and values has been one of the most pivotal factors in my personal and professional development.

It’s also helped me stay resilient when opportunities don’t work out as planned. Instead of being discouraged by a “no,” I try to ask: How can I turn that no into a yes — or a not yet?

That’s something one of my mentors, Milton Newberry, III , recently instilled in me, and it’s allowed me to remain optimistic and confident in my pursuit of future opportunities.

Confidence becomes visible when you take that initial step to speak up — to share who you are, what you value, and what you’re passionate about. That’s what helps you stand out, and ultimately, what helps you move closer to your goals.

So take a moment to practice this in your everyday life:
	•	Introduce yourself to someone new
	•	Start a conversation in the elevator
	•	Initiate conversation with colleagues

Over time, your network will grow beyond what you imagined — and opportunities will follow, all through the simple act of using your voice.
